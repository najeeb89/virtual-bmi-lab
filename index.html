Procedure

1. Switch ON the DC defibrillator and the defibrillator simulator.
2. Connect simulator output to the ECG input of the defibrillator; ECG output to CH1 of the DSO.
3. Select ‘Normal’ on the simulator with mode switch in SYNCH. Connect sync output to CH2 and observe the rising edge of the sync pulse coinciding with the QRS complex.
4. Select ‘A-fib’. Press CHARGE to an energy level (e.g. 300 J); the value appears on the front panel.
5. Connect the ‘Patient’ output to the simulator. In SYNCH mode, discharge and observe the corrected ECG. If not corrected, charge to a higher energy and repeat.
6. Select ‘V-fib’ and repeat with the mode switch in MANUAL.

Result

Thus the working of defibrillator was studied.

    Roller pump with 3/8″ tubing ≈ 27 mL per revolution. Time is accelerated ×10 for temperature changes.

    Observations

    • Venous blood is siphoned from the right atrium / vena cavae into the reservoir by gravity.
    • The roller pump compresses tubing against a backing plate; flow ≈ rpm × stroke volume. Required flow ≈ 2.4 L/min/m² (about 5 L/min in an adult).
    • Bubble oxygenator: blood exposed to 95% O₂ + 5% CO₂ bubbles; defoaming filter removes bubbles. Membrane oxygenator: gas diffuses across fine tubes — FiO₂ sets PaO₂, sweep gas flow sets PaCO₂.
    • Heat exchanger cools the blood (hypothermia lowers O₂ demand) and later rewarms it before weaning off bypass.
    • Arterial filter removes clots and debris before return to the ascending aorta (or femoral artery).

    Thus the working of heart lung machine is studied with a working model.

    Module 03 · Audiometry

    Aim

    To measure the pure tone air conduction and to study bone conduction thresholds of hearing using audiometer.

    Equipment Required

    Present the tone at a level the patient clearly hears, then reduce in 10 dB steps; after no response, raise in 5 dB steps. Record the lowest level heard consistently.

    Audiogram

    O AC RΔ AC R masked< BC R[ BC R masked X AC L□ AC L masked> BC L] BC L masked

    Tabulation

    Record AC at 500, 1k, 2k Hz (and BC) for each ear to get the pure tone average and type of loss.

    Procedure

    Air conduction
    1. Plug the headphones into the jack and switch on the audiometer.
    2. Set the mode to RIGHT or LEFT for the ear to be tested.
    3. Start at 1000 Hz. Present the tone at a higher level so the patient knows what to listen for, then reduce the level until the threshold is reached; note the dB.
    4. Repeat to verify the reliability of the response. Test the higher frequencies, then the frequencies below 1000 Hz.
    Bone conduction
    5. Plug in the bone vibrator (placed on the mastoid) and set the switch to BC.
    6. Test 250–4000 Hz only, by the same method. Mask the non-test ear, since bone-conducted sound reaches both cochleae.

    Observations

    • Degree of loss (PTA of 500, 1k, 2k Hz): Normal ≤ 25 · Mild 26–40 · Moderate 41–55 · Moderately severe 56–70 · Severe 71–90 · Profound > 90 dB HL.
    • Conductive loss: AC raised, BC normal (air–bone gap ≥ 15 dB). Sensorineural: AC and BC both raised, no gap. Mixed: both raised with a gap.
    • Interaural attenuation ≈ 40 dB for headphones and ≈ 0 dB for bone conduction — hence masking.

    Result

    The hearing acuity was assessed using an audiometer, and the results were plotted on an audiogram.
